# Chapter 1: Low Tide

## Outline

1. Nell walks the flats to the wreck her father used to fish from
2. She finds his knife wedged in the ribs of the hull
3. The tide turns and she has to choose what to carry back

---

## Chapter Text

The tide went out further that morning than Nell had ever seen it go. The mud lay open for a mile, ribbed and shining, and out at the edge of it the old wreck stood up on its keel like something that had decided to walk home.

She had not been out to it since the funeral. Her father had fished from its lee for thirty years, standing in water to his thighs, and he had always said the wreck was the only boat on the coast that never needed painting.

The mud took her boots to the ankle with every step. Worms had left their coils everywhere, and the gulls watched her from the posts of the old fish weir without bothering to lift.

At the wreck she put her hand on the nearest rib. The wood was soft and cold and gave a little, like bread. Something caught the light low down, where the planking had gone, and she crouched and found his knife pushed into the timber to the hilt, the way he used to leave it while he baited a line.

She worked it free. The blade was dark but whole.

* * *

Behind her the first of the water had started to come back. It did not arrive as a wave. It simply appeared in the hollows, flat and grey, joining one hollow to the next, and the mud between them went quiet.

Nell stood with the knife in her hand and looked at the wreck. She could leave it where he had put it. She could take it home and put it in the drawer with his watch and his reading glasses and the letters nobody had opened.

The water reached her boots.

She pushed the knife back into the rib, to the hilt, and walked towards the shore without hurrying.
