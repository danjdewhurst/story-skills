For four winters Tomas Reyes has kept Greywidow Light alone, and he has never opened his late wife's sea-chest. Then, one evening at dusk, he finds a brass key hanging on the lamp-room door that could fit no lock but hers.

Tomas is 58 and has tended the light on its rock off the village of Skerry for 31 years. He knows every sound the lamp makes. He does not know who could have climbed his stair to leave a key, or why the only lock it fits is the chest Ana left shut. Petra Lindqvist skippers the supply boat and calls every second Thursday, and Tomas tells her nothing. He keeps the key in his pocket and keeps the light, the way he always has, and tries not to think about the chest.

The Board's letter gives him until the spring relief: the light goes electric, and the keeper goes ashore. Before then Tomas must choose between opening Ana's sea-chest and leaving it shut, knowing that whatever is inside may change the woman he has spent four years mourning, and that once he leaves the rock he may never get the chance again. Whatever he chooses, he will have to live with it alone.
