# Chapter 2: Salt

## Outline

1. Last firing of the pans before the salt works closes
2. Dev and Ruth rake the final crystals
3. Ruth fills a jar for each of the men who worked there

---

## Chapter Text

The last firing of the pans took two days, and on the second evening Ruth and Dev raked the salt together while the light went out of the shed windows.

The crystals came up in hollow pyramids, each one a little upturned roof, and they made a sound against the rake like snow giving under a boot. Dev worked steadily. He had worked these pans since he was fifteen, and his forearms were mapped with small white burns.

"They'll knock it down by Easter," he said. "Flats, probably."

Ruth said nothing. She had come to do the accounts eight years ago and had stayed to do everything else.

When the pans were scraped she fetched the box of jam jars she had been saving since the summer, washed and labelled in her square hand. There were nineteen of them. One for every name on the list pinned inside the office door, the living and the dead, going back to the year the works reopened after the flood.

They filled them in silence. Dev held each jar while Ruth spooned, and when a jar was full he tapped it twice on the bench to settle it, the way you settle flour, and screwed the lid down.

The last jar was his. He looked at the label for a long time.

"You didn't put one for yourself," he said.

"I'm not on the list."

He set his jar down, took an empty one from the box, and held it out to her. Then he picked up the scoop.
