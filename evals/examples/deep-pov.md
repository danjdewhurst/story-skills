The stairs took what was left of my knees. Dusk in the lamp room, the glass going grey, the rock below already gone dark.

The brass key rode in my pocket, cold through the cloth against my leg.

The Fresnel stood in the last of the light and held it after the sky had let go. The paraffin wanted trimming before dark. It had wanted trimming since Thursday.

Ana's sea-chest sat under the spare mantles where it had sat for four winters. Whoever put the key on that door had walked up these same stairs to do it.

Morning would do for all of it. Morning had done for a while now.
