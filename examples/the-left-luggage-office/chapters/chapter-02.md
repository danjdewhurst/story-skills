## Chapter Text

Her mother kept the birthday cards in a biscuit tin on top of the wardrobe, and she did not ask why Ines wanted them.

Ines found the one she was looking for near the bottom. A fox in a waistcoat, and inside, in slanting blue capitals: TO INES, ELEVEN TODAY. ALL MY LOVE, AUNTY FOLA.

She laid the counterfoil beside it on the kitchen table. The same slant. The same crossed I, as if the letter needed holding up.

"Where did you get that?" said Grace Achebe.

"Someone left a suitcase at work last night. A woman in a green coat." Ines watched her mother's face. "She paid for a week. She told me not to open it. And a man came for it an hour later with a ticket I never wrote."

Her mother sat down. She did not often sit down in her own kitchen.

"Fola went to Lagos," she said. "That's what we told people, anyway. The year you were eleven. She had a row with your dad about something at the station, and she went, and she never wrote again." She touched the card, not the counterfoil. "That was her last one."

"What about the station?"

"Your dad never said. Twenty-two years he worked there, and he never told me one thing about it I didn't already know." She pushed the lid back onto the tin. "If that's Fola, she's been somewhere thirteen years without once coming to this door. You don't owe her a suitcase."

Ines didn't answer. She was thinking about the man with the wet shoulders, and the ticket with no pencil tick, and the way he had said *my wife*.

* * *

She got to the office at ten. Pat Dunmore was already in his coat, which was unusual; he liked to hand over slowly, with a list.

"Quiet day," he said. "Couple of rucksacks. One collection off your shelf."

"Off mine?"

"Shelf nine. Ticket was in order." He was halfway to the door. "It's in the book."

Ines opened the collections book. The day's page was in Dunmore's neat hand, and the entry was the last one on it.

*16:10. Ticket 4417. Blue suitcase. Collected by:*

And in the signature box, in slanting blue capitals, with a crossed I as if it needed holding up:

*I. ACHEBE.*
