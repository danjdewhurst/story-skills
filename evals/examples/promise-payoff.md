The key was cold enough to ache. I carried it down the steps with the lamp unlit behind me, and the dark coming on the way it does in October, all at once.

The sea-chest sat where it had sat for four winters, under the spare mantles. Ana's chest. I'd never opened it. The brass key went in without argument, the way a true key does.

Inside: her logbook, the oilskin cover gone soft at the corners, and a coil of fuse wire I didn't remember buying. I lifted the logbook out. Her hand on the first page, the date from the spring before the storm winter. Lamp trimmed. Wind NE. Tomas snoring.

I sat on the floor with it until the light went properly and the room filled with the smell of cold paraffin. I did not light the lamp. Some nights the dark can wait its turn.

Upstairs, the Fresnel stood blind. I put the logbook back, locked the chest, and put the key in my pocket. Whoever left it on the door could come and ask for it.
