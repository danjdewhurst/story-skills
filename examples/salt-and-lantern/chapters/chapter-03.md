# Chapter 3: The Lantern Room

## Outline

1. The estate agent shows a couple round the decommissioned lighthouse
2. The last keeper's daughter has come along uninvited
3. In the lantern room she lights the old lamp one more time

---

## Chapter Text

The estate agent had a laminated sheet of selling points and read from it on every landing. Original features. Panoramic views. Planning permission in principle for a glass extension at the base.

The couple nodded at everything. They were from the city and had brought the wrong shoes.

Clare followed a few steps behind. Nobody had asked her to come, and nobody had asked her to leave. Her father had kept this light for twenty-two years, and she had learned to climb these stairs before she could read, counting them under her breath. There were a hundred and twelve. There still were.

The lantern room was smaller than the couple expected. The great lens sat in the middle of it, tiered glass the colour of weak tea, with the brass gone green where the hands of keepers had not touched it in a decade.

"It's not operational, obviously," the agent said. "The automated beacon's on the pier now."

Clare went to the cupboard under the gallery door. The paraffin was where it had always been. So were the matches, in a tin that had once held boiled sweets.

"I don't think you're supposed to," the agent began.

The wick took slowly. Then the lens caught it and threw it outward, broken into bright blades that walked across the ceiling and the couple's raised faces and out through the glass towards the water, where nothing was waiting for it.

For a moment nobody read anything from any sheet.
