The musicians' gallery smelled of rosin and old dust, and Kael's elbow was in Sera's ribs.

"Move," she whispered.

"You move. You're the tall one."

Below them, the throne room glowed. Ember-light ran through the basalt in slow gold rivers, and her father sat in the middle of it with his hands open on his knees, the way he sat when he meant to say no kindly.

General Maren had not taken off his travelling cloak. Mud from the border road had dried pale on his boots.

"Three passes, Majesty," he said. "We held two. The third we held with bodies. Give me the well for one season and I will give you a border that stays where it is."

"And when the border is safe, General," her father said, "who will you point the embers at next?"

Maren's gauntlet flared. The ember-stone set into the knuckles went bright enough that Sera blinked, then dim, as he closed his fist around it.

"I am not asking you to stop being kind, Majesty. I am asking you to stop being slow."

The court went very quiet. Sera found she was holding Kael's sleeve.

"Come on," Kael breathed. He tugged her backwards into the dark behind the gallery benches, where a panel stood a finger's width open. Chalk marks ran along the stone beyond it. "I found where this goes. All the way down."

"It's just a servants' passage."

"It isn't," he said. "I've touched the gate at the bottom."
