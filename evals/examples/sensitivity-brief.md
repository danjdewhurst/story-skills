## Why I am asking you

The narrator, Tomas Reyes, is 58 and has age-related macular degeneration: his central vision is failing and his side vision is intact, and he hides it from everyone. I am fully sighted and have no lived experience of sight loss, so I cannot see what I may have got wrong.

## Characters and places to focus on

- Tomas Reyes: first-person narrator and protagonist, on every page; his sight loss is on the page in chapters 4, 7, and 11.
- The lighthouse tower and lamp room: chapter 4, where he counts the steps and works the lamp by touch.
- The Board's inspector: chapter 7, a short visit.
- Petra Lindqvist: chapter 11, the first person Tomas tells.

## Specific questions

1. In chapter 4 Tomas counts the 114 steps and keeps the lamp room arranged so he can work it by touch. Is that how someone with his sight loss would cope, or does it ring false?
2. In chapter 7 Tomas bluffs his way through reading the log aloud to the Board's inspector. Does the scene use his sight loss as a cheap source of suspense?
3. Across the three chapters, does the book treat his sight loss as nothing but tragedy?
4. Anything else that pulled you out of the story or felt harmful.

## What I have researched

Two patient-charity leaflets on macular degeneration, and one conversation with an optometrist who asked not to be named. My research note on this is still open; your read is the review it needs.
